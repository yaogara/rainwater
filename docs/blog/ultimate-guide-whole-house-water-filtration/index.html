The Ultimate Guide to Whole-House Water Filtration Systems

By Rainwater Directory Team

•

Updated November 22, 2026

Whole-house (point-of-entry, or POE) water filtration protects appliances, stabilizes taste and clarity at every fixture, and—when combined with validated disinfection—serves as the treatment backbone for indoor non-potable and potable rainwater systems.

Unlike point-of-use (POU) pitcher filters or under-sink faucets that handle a fraction of a gallon per minute, a whole-house rack must deliver peak household flow rates (typically 8–15+ GPM) with minimal pressure loss while meeting exact turbidity requirements before any UV sterilizer.

Typical Whole-House Trains by Water Source

1. Whole-House Potable Rainwater (Where Code Permits)

According to University of Arizona Cooperative Extension (az1863) and ARCSA/ASPE/ANSI Standard 63, potable roof-water relies on layered upstream pre-screening plus fine cartridge and UV barriers:

  1. Roof leaf screen & first-flush diverter + NSF/ANSI 61 opaque cistern with calming inlet and floating intake.
  2. Coarse sediment prefilter (20–50 µm) (washable spin-down or pleated cartridge).
  3. Fine sediment cartridge (5 µm or 1 µm nominal/absolute) — mandatory to strip suspended particles that would shadow microbes from UV light.
  4. Activated carbon block (5 µm or finer, NSF/ANSI 53/42) — improves color/taste and reduces organic compounds.
  5. Calcite neutralizer (if copper/brass piping is present) — stabilizes slightly acidic rainwater against metallic pipe corrosion.
  6. NSF/ANSI 55 Class A UV reactor (minimum 40 mJ/cm² dose) with an audible/visual lamp-fail alarm and optional automatic solenoid shutoff valve.
  7. Post-UV sample port for annual state-certified coliform/E. coli testing.

(See our CDC Potable Rainwater Guide for roof material limits and disinfection details.)

2. Indoor Non-Potable Rainwater (Toilets & Laundry)

Where homes use harvested rainwater only for toilet flushing and washing machines (while keeping municipal or well water on drinking/bathing taps):

  • 20 µm → 5 µm sediment + carbon block keeps toilet fill valves from sticking, stops silt staining in laundry, and minimizes color/odor.
  • UV or chlorination may still be required by state/local plumbing code (e.g., UPC Chapter 16 / ARCSA 63); all distribution piping must use purple pipe or continuous non-potable labeling, and municipal makeup water requires an approved air gap or ASSE 5110 RPZ backflow assembly.

3. Municipal Tap Water & Private Well Water

  • Municipal tap: Usually 5–20 µm sediment → catalytic carbon block (targets chloramine & disinfection byproducts), plus a water softener only if utility water is hard.
  • Private wells: Always run a certified lab water panel first (bacteria, nitrates, arsenic, hardness, iron/manganese, pH) before specifying sediment/carbon, iron oxidation, softening, or Class A UV.

Flow Rate, Housing Size, and Pressure Drop Math

Undersized housings starve upstairs showers and starve UV contact velocity—while oversized UV units run hotter if water stands idle.

  1. Match peak GPM realistically: Count simultaneous fixtures (typically 8–12 GPM for a standard 2–3 bath home, 12–15+ GPM for larger households).
  2. Use 20” × 4.5” (“Big Blue”) housings for whole-house: Small 10” × 2.5” slim cartridges choke quickly above 3–5 GPM and trigger noticeable shower pressure drops within weeks. Dual or triple 20” × 4.5” housings preserve flow and extend cartridge life.
  3. Respect UV maximum rated flow: Never run water faster than the UV chamber’s NSF/ANSI 55 Class A rated GPM; install a flow restrictor upstream of the UV chamber if your booster pump can exceed that rating so every drop receives the full ≥ 40 mJ/cm² UV dose.

Plumbing Layout Tips for Easy Service

  • Full-port ball valves + unions: Isolate the entire treatment skid on unions with a valved bypass loop (for non-potable/utility service—never bypass a potable UV barrier during normal consumption) so housing O-rings and cartridges swap out cleanly without draining house lines.
  • Pressure gauges before and after filters: Two 0–100 PSI glycerin-filled gauges tell you at a glance when a cartridge is loading up (a >10 PSI differential across a stage signals replacement time).
  • Dedicated drain and surge protection: Provide a nearby floor drain or bucket clearance under vertical housings, and wire UV ballasts through a surge protector (voltage spikes frequently damage UV power supplies).
  • Freeze protection: Mount POE skids in a conditioned mechanical room or insulated pump enclosure.
