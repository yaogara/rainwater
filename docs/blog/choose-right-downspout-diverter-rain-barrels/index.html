How to Choose the Right Downspout Diverter for Rain Barrels

By Rainwater Directory Team

•

Published January 12, 2025

The downspout diverter is the small part that makes everything work. Choose one that fits your gutter, handles your rainfall, and simplifies maintenance.

First flush diverter or downspout diverter?

A downspout diverter routes roof runoff into storage and may bypass the tank when it is full. A first flush diverter isolates the earliest runoff, which can carry roof dust, pollen, and debris. Some products combine these functions, but many do not. Check the product diagram rather than assuming a basic barrel kit includes first-flush treatment.

There is no universal diversion volume that fits every roof. Roof material, local dust and wildlife, intended water use, and the length of the dry period all affect the choice. Follow the manufacturer’s sizing instructions and any local health or plumbing rules. A serviceable cleanout and a controlled drain make maintenance easier.

First-flush sizing guidance from university extension

University of Arizona Cooperative Extension publications on rainwater harvesting system components note that diversion volume depends on catchment area, roof material, and local debris loads. Their passive water harvesting fact sheet (az1564) and landscape use guide (az1344) describe first-flush devices as a standard component for diverting initial roof runoff away from storage. For potable-use systems, the 2021 Arizona Extension handout (az1863) emphasizes that pre-filtration and first-flush diversion are critical before sediment filtration and UV disinfection stages. Always size the diverter to your specific roof area and follow the manufacturer’s installation template.

Additional university extension guidance on first-flush volumes:

  • Oklahoma State Extension (BAE-1757): Recommends diverting at least 10 gallons per 1,000 square feet of catchment area. Provides pipe diameter and chamber length specifications for standpipe diverters (3” pipe: 32.75” per gallon; 4”: 18.5”; 6”: 8.25”; 8”: 4.63”).
  • Virginia Tech Extension (BSE-265): Suggests 10–50 gallons per 1,000 ft² of catchment surface depending on debris accumulation, dry days since last rainfall, and intended water use. Each downspout requires its own first-flush diverter sized to its catchment portion.
  • Texas A&M AgriLife Extension: Recommends 1–2 gallons of first-flush diversion per 100 square feet of collection area. Notes that diversion volume varies with dust accumulation, dry days, tree overhang, season, and roof slope.
  • Hawaii CTAHR: Cites 5–10 gallons per 1,000 square feet of roof area for effective contaminant reduction.

Common diverter types

  • In‑line drop‑in: Inserts into vertical downspout; easy retrofits; good for 50–200 gal barrels.
  • Flexible hose kits: Simple installs; watch for kinks and UV aging.
  • High‑flow in‑line boxes: Better for heavy rain and multiple barrels.
  • Y‑diverters with shutoff: Manual control and easy winter bypass.
  • First-flush integrated diverters: Combine downspout diversion with a standpipe or floating-ball chamber that captures initial roof runoff before directing water to the barrel.

Critical fit rules from manufacturer instructions:

  • Amerimax / Home Depot / Lowe’s kits: Most universal kits include separate inserts for 2×3 and 3×4 rectangular downspouts. The diverter body is reversible—narrow end up for 2×3, wide end up for 3×4. The diverter tubing holes must face the barrel.
  • EarthMinded FlexiFit Universal: Rubber diverter body stretches to fit both 2×3 and 3×4 rectangular. A separate “3×4 only” kit exists for larger downspouts; the universal version captures less water on 3×4.
  • Oatey Mystic: Fits 2×3 rectangular only. UV-resistant paintable plastic with 4-foot hose and winter outlet plug.
  • RESCUE Universal (Emsco): Heavy-duty resin construction; includes both 2×3 and 3×4 diverter inserts in the kit.
  • Amerimax Catch-A-Raindrop (Model 37-042): Filter-type diverter for 2×3 only; vinyl construction; accepts garden hose or cap.
  • Clean Rain Advanced: First-flush plus leaf screen; fits 2×3, 3×4, 3” and 4” round with included adapters; plastic body with stainless screen; on/off switch with overflow to drain.

University extension conveyance sizing rules

  • Texas A&M AgriLife Extension (Conveyance): Provide one square inch of downspout area for every 100 square feet of roof area. A 2×3 downspout (6 sq in) accommodates ~600 sq ft roof; a 3×4 downspout (12 sq in) accommodates ~1,200 sq ft roof. Gutters should be at least 5 inches wide and sized for a 100-year storm event.
  • Virginia Tech Extension (BSE-265): Confirms the 1 sq in per 100 sq ft rule. Each downspout requires its own first-flush diverter and screen, sized to its catchment portion.
  • Oklahoma State Extension (BAE-1757): Emphasizes that downspout conveyance must handle peak intensity; first-flush diverter chamber length depends on pipe diameter (see table above).
  • New York State Rainwater Harvesting Guide (citing Texas A&M): Downspout Size (sq in) = (Length × Width of catchment area in feet) / 100.

Selection checklist: measure before you buy

  1. Profile: Rectangular or round? Measure inside width and depth (rectangular) or inside diameter (round).
  2. Material: Aluminum, steel, vinyl, or copper? Vinyl systems require vinyl-specific diverters; copper downspouts should not be drilled for standard diverters.
  3. Corrugation: Smooth or corrugated interior? Some diverters seal only on smooth walls.
  4. Wall thickness: Metal downspouts flare at ends and warp over time—measure at the installation height, not at the elbow.
  5. Kit contents: Verify the kit includes the correct insert/adapters for your measured size. “Universal” often means two separate inserts, not one adjustable body.

Right-sizing for rainfall

  • Estimate peak downspout flow by roof area feeding it and local rainfall intensity.
  • For frequent heavy storms, prefer high‑flow diverters and large, screened overflows.
  • Texas A&M AgriLife Extension: Gutters should be sized for 100-year storm events. A slight increase in pipe or gutter size can drastically increase system cost; accurate sizing matters.

Installation tips

  • Keep the barrel elevated for hose pressure and spigot access.
  • Seal lids; add basket screens and first‑flush if possible.
  • Route overflow away from foundations; equalize levels if daisy‑chaining barrels.
  • Diverter height rule (multiple sources): The diverter outlet must be level with the barrel’s intake hole. Too high = barrel overflows; too low = barrel won’t fill. Use a level to mark the downspout at the height of the barrel intake.
  • Secure mounting: Use self-tapping screws provided. EarthMinded and MMSD instructions specify two screws with the collection funnel facing up and arrow pointing straight up.
  • Winter bypass: Y-diverter or seasonal caps to bypass the barrel. Drain barrels before freeze; store hoses and open valves.

Winterizing

  • Use Y‑diverter or seasonal caps to bypass the barrel.
  • Drain barrels before freeze; store hoses and open valves.
  • EarthMinded FlexiFit kits: Include a winter cover cap for the diverter outlet.
  • Oatey Mystic: Includes an outlet plug for winter or full-barrel shutdown.
