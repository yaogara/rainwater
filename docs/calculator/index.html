How the calculator works

Every rainwater harvesting plan answers two distinct engineering questions: how much runoff your roof generates over a year, and how much storage capacity you need to bridge dry stretches between storms.

1. Annual roof collection formula

Annual Gallons = Roof Area (sq ft) × Rainfall (in) × 0.6233 × Efficiency

One inch of rainfall over one square foot equals 0.6233 U.S. gallons. Typical residential runoff efficiencies range from 0.80 (rough shingle roofs with first-flush losses) to 0.90 (smooth metal or tile roofs).

2. Demand-based dry-spell storage target

Storage Target (gal) = Daily Demand (gal/day) × Reserve Days

A humid climate might supply plenty of annual gallons, yet still need a larger cistern if summers have 30–60 rainless days. Conversely, arid regions often match tank sizing to monsoon bursts and municipal rebate thresholds.

Calculator frequently asked questions

How does the rainwater collection calculator estimate annual gallons?
Annual roof collection uses the standard engineering formula: Roof Area (sq ft) × Annual Rainfall (inches) × 0.6233 gallons per sq ft-inch × Runoff Efficiency (typically 0.80 to 0.90 to account for splash-over, evaporation, leaf screens, and first-flush diversion).
Why does the calculator separate annual yield from storage tank size?
Annual yield shows how much rain your roof can harvest over an entire year, while storage tank size depends on how many gallons per day you use multiplied by the longest dry spell you want to bridge between storms.
Are statewide NOAA rainfall averages accurate for final system engineering?
No. The built-in 1971–2000 NOAA statewide normals are starting screening estimates only. Because rainfall varies sharply across counties and microclimates, select 'Use manual rainfall' and enter local station data before finalizing tank or cistern specs.
Is harvested rainwater safe to drink straight from the tank?
No. Untreated roof runoff is non-potable by default. Drinking rainwater requires multi-stage filtration (including 5-micron sediment and activated carbon) plus validated disinfection such as NSF/ANSI 55 Class A UV where local codes permit.
