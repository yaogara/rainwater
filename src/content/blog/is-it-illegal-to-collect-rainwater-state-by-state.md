Rainwater collection rules depend on the state, the intended use, and sometimes the building or parcel. A rooftop barrel used outdoors can fall under different rules from an indoor or drinking-water system. **Check the water-rights agency, building department, and health authority for your project before installing or connecting a system.**

This guide keeps the existing state-by-state directory route, but only gives specific legal limits where we have checked an official source. Other jurisdictions are marked for source review rather than assigned an unsupported legal category or rebate amount. Puerto Rico is a U.S. territory and appears separately from the 50 states.

### Colorado: residential barrels and the separate well pathway

[House Bill 16-1005](https://www.leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb16-1005) established the two-barrel, 110-gallon combined-capacity residential provision. Colorado's [current agency guidance](https://dwr.colorado.gov/services/water-administration/rainwater-storm-water-graywater) says the barrels can be filled and refilled through the year, need sealable lids, and the collected water is for outdoor use on the property. Do not treat 110 gallons as a universal cap: the agency describes another permit route for eligible properties with residential wells. The well permit determines allowed uses.

### Utah: container size is per container

The [Utah registration page](https://www.waterrights.utah.gov/forms/rainwater.asp) specifies no more than **two covered containers without registration**, neither larger than **100 gallons**. The registered limit is **2,500 gallons total**, registration is free, and the water must be captured and used on the same parcel. The earlier wording that limited unregistered storage to 100 gallons *total* was incorrect.

### Texas: exemptions and restrictions have conditions

The [Texas Water Development Board](https://www.twdb.texas.gov/innovativewater/rainwater/) points readers to the relevant state provisions. [Tax Code § 151.355](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.151.htm#151.355) concerns qualifying equipment and supplies, not an unconditional exemption for every purchase. [Property Code § 202.007](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.202.htm#202.007) restricts some association prohibitions but permits specified rules about visible systems. [Health & Safety Code § 341.042](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/HS/htm/HS.341.htm#341.042) provides safeguards for relevant public-water connections and potable systems. Ask the local authority about the proposed design and permits. Browse the [Texas directory](/states/texas/) or [Austin installer listings](/installers/texas/austin/) while checking each contractor's source status.

### Puerto Rico: what Ley 91-2024 establishes

[Ley Núm. 91-2024](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/91-2024.pdf) establishes public policy to promote rainwater harvesting. It assigns government implementation duties; it does not itself grant a general residential permit exemption or rebate. For the listed health-related uses, it calls for an appropriate system and Department of Health evaluation. The [Puerto Rico directory](/states/puerto-rico/) offers local listing and source-review status.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I drink collected rainwater?

Do not assume a barrel's water is safe to drink. Treatment and approval depend on the source, system, and local health rules. See our [drinking-water guide](/blog/can-you-drink-rainwater-cdc-science/) for a planning overview and ask the relevant health authority before using roof runoff as drinking water.

### Can a homeowners association restrict a rain barrel?

The answer depends on the jurisdiction and the association's governing documents. For example, [Colorado HB16-1005](https://www.leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb16-1005) includes protection with stated exceptions and allows reasonable appearance rules. [Texas Property Code § 202.007](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.202.htm#202.007) also has qualifications. Check the law and documents that apply to your property.

### Do I need a permit?

Rules vary with container size, indoor plumbing, use, and location. Colorado's qualifying two-barrel provision does not require DWR approval, while its separate residential-well pathway does. Utah's unregistered container allowance differs from its registered storage tier. Ask your local building and water authorities about your specific system.
