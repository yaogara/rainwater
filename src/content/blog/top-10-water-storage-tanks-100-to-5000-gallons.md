Choosing the right tank depends on required storage, location, budget, and whether the water is potable. These are common tank types rather than product rankings. Start with the [rainwater planning calculator](/calculator/) to separate annual collection potential from your demand-based storage target.

Quick picks by use case

- Emergency/portable (100–330 gal): Reconditioned IBC tote (food‑grade), small poly barrels.
- Budget irrigation (200–1000 gal): Rotomolded polyethylene (UV‑stabilized) above‑ground tanks.
- Aesthetic/compact: Slimline or decorative polyethylene.
- Large above‑ground (2500–5000+ gal): Corrugated steel with liner; modular and scalable.
- Underground, long life: Concrete or fiberglass cisterns.
- Low‑clearance/indoor crawlspace: Bladder tanks.

## Standards & certifications that matter

Before selecting a tank, verify it meets the standards required for your intended use. The following primary standards govern tank materials, construction, and water-contact safety in the United States:

- **NSF/ANSI/CAN 61** — *Drinking Water System Components: Health Effects* (NSF International). Required by nearly all U.S. states for any tank, liner, or fitting that contacts potable water. Section 4 covers pipes; Section 5 covers barrier materials (liners/coatings); Section 6 covers joining/sealing materials. A tank listed to NSF/ANSI 61 has been tested for contaminant leaching under standardized conditions. See the [NSF official listings for tanks](https://info.nsf.org/Certified/PwsComponents/Listings.asp?ProductType=Tanks&Standard=061).
- **NSF/ANSI/CAN 372** — *Drinking Water System Components: Lead Content*. Verifies lead content compliance per the Safe Drinking Water Act. Often paired with NSF/ANSI 61.
- **ASTM D1998** — *Standard Specification for Polyethylene Upright Storage Tanks* (ASTM International). Covers rotationally molded, one-piece, flat-bottom, cylindrical polyethylene tanks from 500 gallons up. Includes requirements for materials (Type I/II polyethylene), low-temperature impact (dart drop), gel test for crosslinked PE, wall thickness tolerances, and hydrostatic testing. Current edition: D1998-21.
- **ASTM D4097** — *Standard Specification for Contact-Molded Glass-Fiber-Reinforced Thermoset Resin Corrosion-Resistant Tanks* (ASTM International). Covers fiberglass (FRP) tanks fabricated by contact molding for above-ground vertical installation. Specifies resin grades (polyester/vinyl ester), laminate construction, structural design, and chemical resistance. Current edition: D4097-19.
- **AWWA D100** — *Welded Carbon Steel Tanks for Water Storage* (American Water Works Association). Minimum requirements for design, construction, inspection, and testing of welded carbon steel tanks at atmospheric pressure. Current edition: D100-2021.
- **AWWA D102** — *Coating Steel Water-Storage Tanks* (AWWA). Minimum requirements for coating systems, surface preparation, application, and inspection of steel water storage tanks. Current edition: D102-2024.
- **AWWA D103** — *Factory-Coated Bolted Carbon Steel Tanks for Water Storage* (AWWA). Requirements for design, construction, inspection, and testing of bolted, factory-coated steel tanks. Current edition: D103-2019.
- **ACI 350** — *Code Requirements for Environmental Engineering Concrete Structures* (American Concrete Institute). Covers structural design, materials, and construction of concrete liquid-containing structures, including hydrostatic uplift, liquid-tightness, and durability requirements. Chapter 21 addresses ground-supported slabs and tanks. Current edition: ACI CODE-350-20.
- **ARCSA/ASPE/ANSI 63** — *Rainwater Catchment Systems* (American Rainwater Catchment Systems Association / American Society of Plumbing Engineers). Section 4.4.3 specifies that above-grade plastic cisterns shall be listed for the applicable use, protected from direct sunlight or constructed of opaque UV-resistant materials, and installed on a structural pad. Section 4.9 covers potable water applications. Current edition: ARCSA/ASPE/ANSI 63-2020.
- **FDA 21 CFR 177.1520** — *Olefin Polymers* (U.S. FDA). Specifies composition, density, melting point, and extractable fraction limits for polyethylene and polypropylene resins used in food-contact articles. Food-grade polyethylene tanks must use resins complying with item 2.1 (density 0.85–1.00, max 5.5% extractable in n-hexane at 50°C).
- **UN/DOT 49 CFR 178.803** — *Testing and Certification of IBCs* (U.S. Department of Transportation). Performance standards for Intermediate Bulk Containers (IBCs) including vibration, drop, stacking, leakproofness, and hydrostatic pressure tests. Food-grade reconditioned IBCs must additionally meet FDA 21 CFR 177.1520 and document prior edible contents, hot caustic CIP wash, and air-hold pressure test.

Tank types (pros/cons)

1) **Polyethylene (rotomolded)**
- Pros: Affordable, many sizes, simple install; potable models available.
- Cons: UV exposure ages plastic; needs stable pad and sun protection.
- **Applicable standards**: ASTM D1998 (construction, impact, wall thickness); NSF/ANSI 61 + 372 (for potable models); FDA 21 CFR 177.1520 item 2.1 (food-grade resin); ARCSA/ASPE/ANSI 63 §4.4.3 (opaque/UV-resistant, listed for use, structural pad).

2) **Slimline/decorative poly**
- Pros: Fits tight spaces, better curb appeal.
- Cons: Usually higher cost per gallon; limited large sizes.
- **Applicable standards**: ASTM D1998; NSF/ANSI 61 + 372 (for potable); FDA 21 CFR 177.1520; ARCSA/ASPE/ANSI 63 §4.4.3.

3) **Corrugated steel with liner**
- Pros: Scales to very large volumes; strong; serviceable liner replacement.
- Cons: Higher upfront cost; requires flat pad; aesthetic/HOA considerations.
- **Applicable standards**: AWWA D103 (bolted factory-coated steel tanks); NSF/ANSI 61 (liner must be listed as barrier material under Section 5); AWWA D102 (coating systems if field-coated).

4) **Fiberglass (above or underground)**
- Pros: Durable, inert; good for underground installs.
- Cons: Cost; may require professional install.
- **Applicable standards**: ASTM D4097 (contact-molded FRP tanks); ASTM D3299 (filament-wound FRP tanks, consolidating D4097); NSF/ANSI 61 + 372 (for potable).

5) **Concrete cistern (underground)**
- Pros: Long service life; buffers pH; traffic‑rated options.
- Cons: Heavy equipment, excavation, and permitting.
- **Applicable standards**: ACI 350 (Ch. 21 for ground-supported liquid-containing structures); ACI 350.1 (tightness testing); NSF/ANSI 61 (if potable, interior coatings/liners must comply).

6) **Bladder tank**
- Pros: Fits crawlspaces; low profile; fast to deploy.
- Cons: Requires protective surface; puncture risk; limited very large sizes.
- **Applicable standards**: NSF/ANSI 61 (bladder material as barrier under Section 5); manufacturer's rated pressure/burst specifications.

7) **Reconditioned IBC tote (275–330 gal)**
- Pros: Widely available; inexpensive per gallon.
- Cons: Verify food‑grade and prior contents; add opaque cover to block light.
- **Applicable standards**: UN/DOT 49 CFR 178.803 (design-type testing, recertification every 2.5 years); FDA 21 CFR 177.1520 (HDPE bottle resin); Food-grade reconditioning protocol (edible prior contents documented, 4-stage hot caustic CIP, air-hold at 3 psi, new EPDM gasket); NSF/ANSI 61 (if potable — note: reconditioned totes do not carry NSF listing).

8) **Galvanized steel (lined vs. unlined)**
- Pros: Rugged; farmhouse aesthetic.
- Cons: Unlined tanks not for potable storage; corrosion concerns.
- **Applicable standards**: AWWA D102 (coating systems); NSF/ANSI 61 (liner as barrier material under Section 5); ASTM A123/A123M (hot-dip galvanizing).

9) **Modular plastic panels/cells**
- Pros: Hidden under decks/landscapes; customizable shapes.
- Cons: Installation complexity; geotextile/liner details matter.
- **Applicable standards**: NSF/ANSI 61 (panel material and liner); manufacturer's structural engineering documentation.

10) **Wooden tanks with liners**
- Pros: Unique look; replaceable liners.
- Cons: Maintenance; not common in all regions.
- **Applicable standards**: NSF/ANSI 61 (liner as barrier material under Section 5); ARCSA/ASPE/ANSI 63 §4.4.3 (structural pad, UV protection); AWWA D102 if steel bands/roof.

Buyer's checklist

- Potable rating and documentation (if drinking): Confirm NSF/ANSI 61 + 372 listing for the specific tank model and liner.
- Opaque walls, sealed lids, screened vents/overflows (ARCSA/ASPE/ANSI 63 §4.4.3.c–d).
- Pad/foundation requirements and seismic/wind loads (ASTM D1998; ARCSA/ASPE/ANSI 63 §4.4.3.f; ACI 350 Ch. 21).
- Fittings, unions, and future service access.
- Freeze, sun, and algae mitigation strategies.
