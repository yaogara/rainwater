Texas provides one of the strongest statutory frameworks for rainwater harvesting in the United States. Multiple state codes work together to protect the right to collect rainwater, provide financial incentives, and establish safety standards for systems connected to public water supplies.

## Statutory Foundation

**Sales Tax Exemption — Texas Tax Code § 151.355**  
Qualifying rainwater-harvesting equipment and supplies are exempt from state sales tax. The exemption covers tanks, cisterns, gutters, downspouts, filters, pumps, and related components when purchased for a rainwater harvesting system. Buyers must provide the seller a completed exemption certificate (Form 01-339) at the time of purchase. The Texas Comptroller publishes guidance confirming the scope of eligible equipment.

**Property Owner Protections — Texas Property Code § 202.007**  
Property owners' associations may not prohibit rainwater harvesting systems. They may impose limited regulations on visible systems' size, type, shielding, and materials, but an outright ban is unenforceable. This statute applies to all residential property owners associations governed by Chapter 202.

**Public Health Safeguards — Texas Health & Safety Code § 341.042**  
For systems connected to a public water supply and used for potable purposes, the installer and maintainer must hold specified plumbing and water-supply-protection credentials. Auxiliary water supplies must meet statutory cross-connection safeguards and applicable health standards. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) adopts rules implementing these requirements.

**State Building Design — Texas Government Code § 447.004(c)(8)**  
Rainwater harvesting is addressed in the design of certain state buildings, reflecting legislative intent to promote the practice in public infrastructure.

## Official Guidance & Verified Incentives

The **Texas Water Development Board (TWDB)** publishes statewide rainwater harvesting guidance covering tax provisions, property-law protections, and system planning resources. TWDB does not administer rebates directly but documents the legal framework and links to local programs.

The **City of Austin** operates a capacity-based rainwater harvesting rebate through Austin Water, offering up to $5,000 for qualifying systems. Eligibility requires an active Austin Water account; current program terms, pre-approval requirements, and funding availability must be confirmed before purchasing equipment. This program is subject to annual budget allocations and may change.

## Practical Implications for System Owners

1. **Tax savings**: Present a completed Form 01-339 to your supplier to claim the sales tax exemption on all qualifying components.
2. **HOA compliance**: If your HOA challenges your system, reference Property Code § 202.007. They may regulate appearance but cannot prohibit collection.
3. **Potable systems**: Any system tied to a municipal supply for drinking water must be installed and maintained by a credentialed professional meeting Health & Safety Code § 341.042 requirements, including approved backflow prevention.
4. **Local permits**: Beyond state statutes, always verify city and county building, plumbing, and electrical permit requirements before installation.

## Climate Context

Texas averages approximately 28 inches of annual precipitation statewide, with significant regional variation (over 50 inches in East Texas, under 15 inches in West Texas).
